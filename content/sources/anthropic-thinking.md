## Summary

Anthropic's overview of Claude's thinking as of 2026-09. With thinking on, Claude works through a problem in thinking blocks before the answer. Those tokens are billed as output and count toward `max_tokens`, even if you don't see them. What you see is at most a summary, never the raw reasoning. On the newest models thinking is on by default and adaptive (Claude decides whether and how much to think), and the `effort` parameter steers how much work goes into the whole response.

## Key claims

- The problem thinking solves. "A model that answers in a single pass has to get everything right on the first try: no scratch work, no checking, no changing course halfway through." (intro)
- What Claude does when thinking. "it restates what is being asked, tries approaches, checks intermediate results, and abandons paths that do not hold up." (intro)
- Where it helps. "This is why thinking improves performance on complex tasks like math, coding, analysis, and long-running agentic work" (intro)
- The cost. "the tokens Claude spends reasoning are billed as output tokens, even when the thinking text isn't returned to you, and they count toward max_tokens alongside the response text." (intro)
- You never see the raw chain of thought. "what you see is never the raw chain of thought: the text in a thinking block is a summary of Claude's reasoning." (How thinking works)
- Summaries are made by a different model. "Summarization is processed by a different model from the one you target in your requests." (Summarized thinking)
- You pay for the full thinking, not the summary. "You're charged for the full thinking tokens generated by the original request, not the summary tokens." (Summarized thinking)
- Hiding thinking (display "omitted") cuts latency, not cost. "Omitting reduces latency, not cost." (Controlling thinking display)
- On Opus 5.5, Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and newer Fable/Mythos models, thinking is already on and needs no configuration. "thinking is already on and needs no configuration." (Configuring thinking)
- On Opus 4.6–4.8 and Sonnet 4.6, thinking is off until you set adaptive mode, which "lets Claude decide when and how deeply to think based on the request." (Configuring thinking)
- Some models can't turn thinking off: Opus 5.5 and the Fable/Mythos 5.x models reject thinking disabled; Sonnet 5 can turn it off. "Thinking can't be turned off on these models." (Turning thinking off)
- Adaptive thinking can skip thinking on simple requests, and then no thinking block comes back. "the model skips thinking for a simple request, no thinking block is produced" (Controlling thinking display)
- Effort controls how much work, including thinking. "the effort parameter controls how much work Claude puts into the whole response, which in adaptive mode includes how often and how deeply it thinks." (Thinking and effort)
- To cut cost or latency, lower effort first. "Lower cost or latency on a thinking-enabled workload: lower effort first." (Thinking and effort)
- Effort is a hint; max_tokens is the hard cap. "Effort is soft guidance. max_tokens is a strict limit." (Thinking and effort)
- Thinking in the current turn takes context space. "Current-turn thinking always counts toward max_tokens , is billed as output tokens, and occupies context window space for the turn that generated it." (Thinking and the context window)
- Asking for the reasoning in the answer text can be refused on some models: "a request that attempts to elicit the model's internal reasoning as part of the response text can be refused" (Summarized thinking)
- The older fixed-budget mode (budget_tokens) is legacy. From the Extended thinking page linked here: "Claude 4.7 and later models do not support it" (Extended thinking (legacy), linked page)
