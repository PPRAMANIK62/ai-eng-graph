## Summary

The paper that named and popularized "LLM-as-a-judge". It defines three ways to use a model as a grader (pairwise comparison, single answer grading, reference-guided grading), measures the judges' biases (position, verbosity, self-enhancement, weak math grading), proposes fixes, and then shows GPT-4's verdicts agree with human experts about as often as experts agree with each other. NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks track. The judges tested (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claude-v1) are old; the method and the bias names are what lasted. Read from the arXiv PDF.

## Key claims

- The headline: "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans." (Abstract)
- Three ways to judge. Pairwise: "An LLM judge is presented with a question and two answers, and tasked to determine which one is better or declare a tie." Single answer grading: "an LLM judge is asked to directly assign a score to a single answer." Reference-guided: "it may be beneficial to provide a reference solution if applicable." (3.1)
- Tradeoffs: pairwise "may lack scalability when the number of players increases, given that the number of possible pairs grows quadratically"; single answer grading "may be unable to discern subtle differences between specific pairs, and its results may become unstable, as absolute scores are likely to fluctuate more than relative pairwise results if the judge model changes." (3.1)
- Benefits: "LLM-as-a-judge offers two key benefits: scalability and explainability." (3.2)
- Position bias: "Position bias is when an LLM exhibits a propensity to favor certain positions over others." In a test with two similar answers, GPT-4 gave the same verdict after swapping the order in 65.0% of cases, GPT-3.5 in 46.2%, Claude-v1 in 23.8% (default prompt; Claude-v1 favored the first answer 75.0% of the time). "Only GPT-4 outputs consistent results in more than 60% of cases." (3.3, Table 2)
- Verbosity bias: "an LLM judge favors longer, verbose responses, even if they are not as clear, high-quality, or accurate as shorter alternatives." A "repetitive list" attack (a numbered list padded with a rephrased copy of its own items, no new information) fooled Claude-v1 and GPT-3.5 in 91.3% of 23 cases and GPT-4 in 8.7%. (3.3, Table 3)
- Self-enhancement: "LLM judges may favor the answers generated by themselves." "GPT-4 favors itself with a 10% higher win rate; Claude-v1 favors itself with a 25% higher win rate." But: "our study cannot determine whether the models exhibit a self-enhancement bias." (3.3)
- Math grading: GPT-4 can be "misled by the provided answers" even on problems it can solve alone. Failure rate on 10 math questions (20 judgments): default 14/20, chain-of-thought 6/20, reference-guided 3/20. (3.3, 3.4, Table 4)
- The swap fix: "call a judge twice by swapping the order of two answers and only declare a win when an answer is preferred in both orders. If the results are inconsistent after swapping, we can call it a tie." (3.4)
- Few-shot examples raised GPT-4's consistency "from 65.0% to 77.5%. However, high consistency may not imply high accuracy". (3.4)
- Agreement: "The agreement under setup S2 (w/o tie) between GPT-4 and humans reaches 85%, which is even higher than the agreement among humans (81%)." (4.2) Random agreement in that setup is 50% (Table 5).
- Humans shown GPT-4's reasoning "deemed GPT-4’s judgments reasonable in 75% of cases and are even willing to change their choices in 34% of cases." (4.2)
- Agreement depends on how different the two models are: it rises "from 70% to nearly 100%" as the win-rate gap grows. "GPT-4 aligns with humans better when significant performance differences exist between the models." (4.2, Figure 2)
- Single answer grading with GPT-4 "may sometimes perform slightly worse than pairwise comparison and give more tie votes, it is a more scalable method." (4.2)
- The single answer grading prompt asks the judge to explain first, then "rate the response on a scale of 1 to 10". (Figure 6, Appendix)

## My notes

- The "over 80%" is agreement on non-tied votes, on chat answers from 2023 models, with random at 50%. It's not a general accuracy number for any judge on any task.
