## Summary

The classic usability note on how long people will wait: 0.1 second feels instant, 1 second keeps the flow of thought, 10 seconds is about the limit of attention. Past that, show progress. Not about LLMs, but it's why the first token matters so much.

## Key claims

- 0.1 s. "is about the limit for having the user feel that the system is reacting instantaneously" (The 3 response-time limits)
- 1 s. "is about the limit for the user's flow of thought to stay uninterrupted" (same)
- 10 s. "is about the limit for keeping the user's attention focused on the dialogue" (same)
- Past 1 s the delay is noticed, even if the flow of thought holds. "even though the user will notice the delay." (1.0 second bullet)
- Past 10 s people turn to other things. "For longer delays, users will want to perform other tasks while waiting for the computer to finish." (10 seconds bullet)
- Under 0.1 s no feedback is needed. "no special feedback is necessary except to display the result." (0.1 second bullet)
- Show progress for long operations. "percent-done progress indicators should be used for operations taking more than about 10 seconds." (same)
- When the length isn't known, show work done or at least that something is happening: "a spinning ball, a busy bee flying over the screen, dots printed on a status line, or any such mechanism that at least indicates that the system is working." (same)
