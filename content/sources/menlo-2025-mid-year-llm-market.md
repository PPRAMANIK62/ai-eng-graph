## Summary

A survey of 150 technical leaders at startups and enterprises (2025-06-30 to 2025-07-10) about which models they run in production and why. Most teams stay with their vendor and upgrade to its newest model; they pay for performance rather than chase cheaper older models. Open-source share of workloads fell from 19% to 13% in six months. Primary for its survey data, but Menlo is an investor in Anthropic and OpenRouter, among others, and the report says so.

## Key claims

- Survey size and dates. "a survey of 150 technical decision-makers at enterprises and startups building AI applications, conducted from June 30 to July 10, 2025." (footnote 2)
- Few switch vendors. "66% of builders upgraded models within their existing provider, while 23% did not switch models at all this past year. Only 11% switched vendors." (Enterprises Switch Models for Performance, Not Price)
- Performance beats price. "Builders consistently choose frontier models over cheaper, faster alternatives. They prioritize and pay for performance." (same)
- Switching is fast when a new model lands: within a month of Claude 4's release, Claude 4 Sonnet had 45% of Anthropic users and Sonnet 3.5 fell from 83% to 16%. (same)
- The quote. "Even as individual models drop 10x in price, builders don't capture savings by using older models; they just move en masse to the best performing one." (same)
- Open-source share. "Thirteen percent of AI workloads today use open-source models, down slightly from 19% six months ago." (Open-Source Adoption in the Enterprise Flattens)
- Why open models are attractive. "Open-source models offer clear enterprise advantages: greater customization, potential cost savings, and the ability to deploy within private cloud or on-premises environments." (same)
- The gap as they see it. "open-source has continued to trail frontier, closed-source models in performance by nine to 12 months." (same)
- Reasons for stagnating share: the performance gap, "the technical complexity of deploying open-source models and enterprise reluctance to use APIs from Chinese companies". (same)
- Market share is usage, not spend: "LLM market share reflects the proportion of production AI usage, not spend." (footnote 3)
