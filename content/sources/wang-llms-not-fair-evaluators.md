## Summary

Shows that GPT-4 and ChatGPT, used as judges comparing two answers, change their verdict when you swap the order of the answers, even when the prompt tells them not to let order matter. The effect is biggest when the two answers are close in quality. Proposes three fixes: have the judge write its reasons before the score, run both orders and average, and send the most order-sensitive cases to a person. Read from the arXiv PDF (v2, 2023-08-30).

## Key claims

- The finding: "the quality ranking of candidate responses can be easily hacked by simply altering their order of appearance in the context." (Abstract)
- The example: "Vicuna-13B could beat ChatGPT on 66 over 80 tested queries with ChatGPT as an evaluator." (Abstract)
- Table 2, Vicuna-13B vs ChatGPT: with GPT-4 judging, Vicuna's win rate was 51.3% when shown first and 23.8% when shown second, with conflicting verdicts on 37 of 80 questions (46.3%). With ChatGPT judging, 2.5% vs 82.5%, conflicts on 66 of 80 (82.5%). Vicuna vs Alpaca with GPT-4 judging: 92.5% either way, 4 conflicts. (Table 2)
- Direction differs by judge: "GPT-4 exhibits a preference for the first displayed candidate response", while ChatGPT "typically favors the second response." (1 Introduction)
- The prompt already warned against it: order flipped results "even though we add the command “ensuring that the order in which the responses were presented does not affect your judgment” into the prompt." (Figure 1 caption)
- Close calls are the unstable ones: when "the score difference between the two responses is small (e.g., score gap ≤ 1)", GPT-4's results were unstable, while with a gap of 3 or more "GPT-4’s evaluation results are relatively stable." (2.2, Figure 2)
- Fixes: "Multiple Evidence Calibration, which requires the evaluator model to generate multiple evaluation evidence before assigning ratings"; "Balanced Position Calibration, which aggregates results across various orders to determine the final score"; "Human-in-the-Loop Calibration" which "seeks human assistance when needed." (Abstract)
- Why reasons first: with a template that gives the verdict first, "the conclusions generated by the model are not supported by the explanation generated afterward." (3.1)
- Results: MEC and BPC improved agreement with human labels "by 9.8% and 14.3% accuracy" for GPT-4 and ChatGPT; with "only a 20% human annotation cost", the judges matched or beat average human alignment. (1 Introduction)
