## Summary

UX guidance on the three ways chat interfaces "explain" answers: source citations, step-by-step reasoning, and disclaimers. Each can backfire by making users trust the answer more than they should. For citations: they're often wrong or unrelated, users rarely click them, so show them right next to the claim, with real labels, styled apart from the answer. For disclaimers: specific, near the input box, and paired with an action.

## Key claims

- Explanations can raise trust whether or not they're right. "Users who are not aware of the limitations see a confident and plausible explanation and automatically trust the output." (Explainable AI)
- Citations can be fake or unrelated. "citations are often hallucinated and point to nonexistent URLs. And, even when the links are real, they may lead to unreliable sources or to unrelated articles that do not actually support the claim made in the AI output." (Source Citations)
- Their research: "people rarely click citation links". One participant said they trusted the chatbot because they could click the source; "Yet that participant never clicked on any of the citations presented during the session." (Source Citations)
- Placement. "Place sources directly next to the specific claim or sentence they support." (Source Citations, guidelines)
- Other citation guidelines: tell users to verify sources; "Style citations differently from the main output response."; don't bury them; "Link to the relevant part of the source."; show meaningful labels like the publication or article title. (Source Citations, guidelines)
- Examples: Copilot shows sources as clickable chips with a hover preview; Perplexity puts inline citation tags next to the text. (Source Citations)
- Step-by-step reasoning shown to users is often unfaithful, so avoid "step-by-step explanations that imply certainty or transparency". (Step-by-step explanations)
- Disclaimers only help if read; people skim and "skip over fine print entirely". Put them near the input box, in clear language, and "Pair disclaimers with an action." (Disclaimers)
