## Summary

A small usability study of site chatbots (9 people, 8 bots). People type short, blunt queries and want the answer first, short and scannable. Long, unformatted answers put them off, and streaming a long answer made one participant feel overwhelmed. When a bot can't help, it should say so in the first line instead of padding.

## Key claims

- Study. "9 people with varying levels of digital and AI literacy interacted with 8 different site-specific AI chatbots." (Users Type Short, Imperfect Queries)
- Streaming a dense answer. One bot "streamed its response (i.e., it displayed its answer incrementally as it was generated)". The participant: "The pouring in of information kind of made me feel overwhelmed". (Make Answers Concise and Easy to Scan)
- "Streaming long responses can intensify the sense of overload, especially when the answer is already too dense to scan." (same section)
- Small viewport. "the chat viewport is much smaller, so even a small paragraph can look like a wall of text." (same section)
- Rule of thumb. "The first answer should include only what the user asked for and any caveats needed to keep that answer accurate." (Give Answers First; Provide Detail on Demand)
- Can't help? "When a chatbot cannot help, do not pad the gap." (When It Can’t Help, Say So Plainly)
- Turo example: the bot couldn't search cars but answered with long instructions on searching; the user misread them and "complained about the time he had wasted." (same section)
- Redfin example: the useful alternative (filter by school rating) sat in the second paragraph; the user "read the first sentence, understood that the chatbot could not help, and stopped." (same section)
- "Participants were disappointed when they were told to review other pages or given vague responses." (Prioritize Specific Answers over Generic Ones)
- Users want answers, not referrals. "Where possible, ensure bots have access to the appropriate information so that they can answer users' questions directly, rather than sending them elsewhere." (Prioritize Specific Answers over Generic Ones)
