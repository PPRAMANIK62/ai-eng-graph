## Summary

OpenAI's own study of consumer ChatGPT usage from launch (November 2022) to July 2025, using an automated, privacy-preserving classifier over a sample of conversations. Most use is now non-work. Practical guidance, seeking information and writing make up nearly 80% of conversations; programming is a small share. Only the NBER page and abstract were read.

## Key claims

- Scale and period. "We document the growth of ChatGPT’s consumer product from its launch in November 2022 through July 2025, when it had been adopted by around 10% of the world’s adult population." (abstract)
- Non-work use grew faster. "non-work-related messages, which have grown from 53% to more than 70% of all usage." (abstract)
- Top three topics. "“Practical Guidance,” “Seeking Information,” and “Writing” are the three most common topics and collectively account for nearly 80% of all conversations." (abstract)
- Writing leads at work. "Writing dominates work-related tasks, highlighting chatbots’ unique ability to generate digital outputs compared to traditional search engines." (abstract)
- Coding is small in consumer ChatGPT. "Computer programming and self-expression both represent relatively small shares of use." (abstract)
- Where the value is. "ChatGPT provides economic value through decision support, which is especially important in knowledge-intensive jobs." (abstract)
- Early users skewed male; the gap narrowed, with faster growth in lower-income countries. "Early adopters were disproportionately male but the gender gap has narrowed dramatically" (abstract)
